[h1]Traditional Preservation (Dairy)[/h1]

Set your own yogurt the old way. Bring milk to the steam in a Hearth's copper, let it cool in a lidded clay pot, add a spoonful of the last batch and wrap it in cloth overnight. It ends in the real vanilla Yogurt, and every cup eaten comes back to be filled again. Lost your last yogurt? Grow a new starter from wheat and warm milk.

[b]Build 42 only (42.22 or newer). This is an early release, so back up your save before enabling it. Singleplayer only: multiplayer is not supported.[/b]

Traditional Preservation (Dairy) requires [b]Traditional Preservation (Core)[/b] (TP Core for short): subscribe to it too. The Hearth comes with TP Core.

[h2]Features[/h2]
[list]
[*]A Yogurt Incubator thrown on the pottery wheel and wrapped in cloth, with its own art for every stage
[*]Milk scalded in the 60 L copper of TP Core's Hearth and poured straight in, or scalded in a pot on any lit stove or fire and carried over, hot for hours in a Wrapped Yogurt Pot, or any pot, jar or bucket you wrap in a sheet (keep the cloth off the heat: it burns)
[*]Milk goes off: raw milk sours and then rots, scalded milk never sours, a fridge slows both, and soured milk still bakes
[*]Real temperatures: the milk cools, the wrap goes on at 45 C, and it sets in about 6 hours when kept warm
[*]Cow's and sheep's milk, alone or mixed, as a mixed herd's always was
[*]Collect into glass jars (1 L) or clay jars (2.5 L) and eat from the jar, 250 ml at a time, or spoon it into cups of vanilla Yogurt that keep the batch's age
[*]Every Yogurt eaten leaves its Empty Yogurt Container
[*]A Starter Culture Crock grows a new starter from wheat and milk, and stands on a counter, a table or the floor
[*]Set it by the fire in winter: a lit fire within 2 tiles keeps a pot at 28 C in any month
[*]Keeps working while you are away
[/list]

[h2]The loop[/h2]
[olist]
[*][b]Pour into the Copper[/b] (the Hearth's right-click menu): up to 60 L of milk from any container.
[*][b]Light the fire[/b]: 10 L steams (85 C) in about 1.5 hours, 20 L in about 2, a full 60 L in about 2.75. Hearth Info shows when.
[*][b]Pour from the Hearth[/b] into a Yogurt Incubator within 2 tiles, up to 5 L. No Hearth yet? Scald a pot of milk on any lit stove or fire and use [b]Pour Scalded Milk[/b] while it's hot.
[*][b]Add Starter[/b]: one vanilla Yogurt, one portion of a Jar of Yogurt, or one Active Yogurt Starter.
[*][b]Wait[/b]: the milk cools to 45 C in about 2 hours, the incubator wraps itself, and the milk sets in about 6 hours at 40 to 45 C.
[*][b]Collect[/b] into empty jars, glass 1 L or clay 2.5 L, or straight into Empty Yogurt Containers.
[*][b]Eat a Portion[/b] from a Jar of Yogurt: 250 ml, with a quarter litre of its milk's food (154 kcal from cow's milk, 206 from sheep's); 4 in a glass jar, 10 in a clay one.
[*][b]Fill a Yogurt Cup[/b] from a Jar of Yogurt: a portion into a cup, a vanilla Yogurt with the jar's food.
[/olist]

[h2]No yogurt left?[/h2]
Load the crock with 20 wheat grains and half a litre of milk at 35 to 45 C. In about a day it cultivates; feed it half a litre of fresh milk, and 12 hours later collect two Active Yogurt Starters. Half a litre of soured milk does the wheat's work at once: no grain, no day's wait. Each sets one batch. Keep it warm: by the fire it takes a day in any month, outdoors in winter weeks.

[h2]Temperature[/h2]
[list]
[*][b]By a fire[/b]: a lit fire within 2 tiles (the Hearth, a fireplace, an antique stove or a campfire) holds 28 C. Yogurt sets in about 6 hours all year. A banked Hearth's or Firebox's embers hold 24 C.
[*][b]Indoors[/b] or [b]under an open roof[/b]: the last 10 days' outdoor average (22 C while the grid is on). Sets from June to November.
[*][b]In a loft[/b] (the top storey: a room upstairs with no room over it): as indoors, plus three tenths of whatever the 10-day average is above 10 C: the sun on the roof (about 24 C in a 21 C July, no warmer than a room from October to April); 22 C while the power grid is on.
[*][b]In a basement[/b]: a month behind the season, about 5 C from January to March, 13 C from July to September. Sets from May to December.
[*][b]Outdoors[/b]: only on warm summer nights.
[/list]
If the milk falls under 40 C before a starter is in, it is too cold: pour it out and reheat it in the copper or on a stove. Once the starter is in, milk too cold to set is refused.

[h2]Good to know[/h2]
[list]
[*]Only scalded milk goes into an incubator: milk that has been at 85 C in the copper or on a stove or fire, or store milk poured out of its carton. A Wrapped Yogurt Pot carries 5 L and keeps it hot for hours; an open pot cools in minutes.
[*]Raw milk sours in about a day and a half at 20 C (3 days in a basement, 8 in a powered fridge, never frozen) and is rotten as long again after. Scalded milk never sours and rots at the same age. Sealed store milk keeps until it is poured out.
[*]Soured milk still bakes: vanilla cake batter, muffins and cereal now take cow's, sheep's or animal milk, fresh, scalded or soured.
[*]A Jar of Yogurt stays fresh 10 days; the cups keep its age.
[*]No loot table is changed. Vanilla's milks now read "(Fresh)" and go off, five vanilla recipes take more kinds of milk (cake batter, muffins, cereal, butter, mildew cure), and vanilla Yogurt gains its empty container and a real cup's food (a quarter litre of cow's milk: 154 kcal, was 30); Yogurt already in a save keeps its old values. A food mod that also changes Yogurt may conflict: the last one loaded wins.
[*]The tiledef number 5172 must be unique among your active mods.
[/list]

[h2]Sandbox options[/h2]
Set the yogurt setting time, the starter culture time, the temperature sensitivity (Off to Harsh), whether milk goes off, and how fast.

[h2]A little history[/h2]
Herders from the Balkans to the steppe made yogurt for thousands of years without a thermometer. Milk was heated until it steamed, cooled until a finger could stand it for a count of ten, given a spoonful of yesterday's yogurt and wrapped in a blanket or set by the embers overnight. A household that lost its starter grew a new one from a few wheat kernels in warm milk.
