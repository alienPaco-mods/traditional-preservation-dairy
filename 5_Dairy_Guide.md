## 1. What This Mod Does

In vanilla, yoghurt comes only from fridges, and once the town is picked clean it's gone. Your cow or sheep keeps giving milk, but there's nothing to do with it except drink it or churn it.

This mod lets you **set your own yoghurt the old way**: milk brought to the steam in a Hearth's copper, cooled in a lidded clay pot, then wrapped in cloth to set overnight. The result is ordinary vanilla **Yoghurt**, so it works in every recipe that uses it. Every Yoghurt eaten leaves its cup behind to fill again, and when the last spoonful of starter is gone, a crock of wheat and warm milk grows a new one.

## 2. Quick Start

> **Requires TP Core** (Traditional Preservation Core), the series' shared mod. Enable it alongside this one; it loads first. The Hearth is part of TP Core.

| Step | You need | You get |
|---|---|---|
| Build a **Hearth** | 12 Clay Bricks, 4 Clay, 1 Copper Sheet, a trowel (kept). Masonry 2 | A fireplace with a 10 L copper |
| Throw a **Yoghurt Pot** and fire it | 6 Clay, a little water. Pottery 2, wheel and kiln | The pot |
| Build a **Yoghurt Incubator** within 2 tiles of the Hearth | 1 Yoghurt Pot, 2 Ripped Sheets, 1 Twine. Tailoring 1 | The incubator |
| **Pour into the Copper** and light the fire | Up to 10 L of cow's or sheep's milk, firewood | Steaming milk in **about 1.5 hours** |
| **Pour from the Hearth**, then **Add Starter** | 1 vanilla Yoghurt or 1 Active Yoghurt Starter | 5 L cooling in the pot |
| Wait **about 8 hours** (2 cooling, 6 setting, in a 20 C room) | | 5 L of yoghurt |
| **Collect** | Empty jars or Empty Yoghurt Containers | Jars of Yoghurt or vanilla Yoghurts |

## 4. Walkthrough

1. **Keep a cow or sheep in milk.** Cow's and sheep's milk both work, and mix.
2. **Build the Hearth** from the Build menu and stack firewood by it.
3. **Make the Yoghurt Pot** on the pottery wheel and fire it in a kiln.
4. **Build the Yoghurt Incubator** from the Build menu, within 2 tiles of the Hearth.
5. **Pour into the Copper** (right-click the Hearth) from your milk buckets, then **Light** the fire.
6. Check **Hearth Info**. When it reads "Yes: steaming", the milk is at 85 C.
7. Right-click the incubator: **Pour from the Hearth**, then **Add Starter** with a Yoghurt you kept back.
8. Go to bed. The lid stays on while it cools, and at 45 C the cloth wraps itself on.
9. In the morning the status reads **Ready**. **Collect into a Jar** or **Fill Empty Yoghurt Containers**.
10. Keep one Yoghurt back for the next batch.

That's it. A full copper is two pots, 10 L of yoghurt from one firing.

## 5. The Hearth's Copper

The Hearth is TP Core's station: a vanilla fireplace (Add Fuel, Light, a firebox that cooks, room heat) with a 10 L copper set in its top. Right-click it for **Pour into the Copper** and **Hearth Info**.

- **One fluid at a time.** Cow's and sheep's milk count as one: they mix freely, and a blend keeps its shares in the copper, in a container and in the pot. Any other mix is refused, shown in red with the reason.
- **Heat mixes by volume.** Cold milk poured onto hot cools the lot.
- **The fire heats the copper towards 95 C.** A full copper goes from 20 C to 85 C in about 1.5 hours; less milk heats faster.
- **With the fire out it cools slowly**, over a few hours, as the stone holds heat.
- **Bank the Fire** burns a third of the wood and holds the copper at 50 C. **Stoke the Fire** brings it back up.
- **At 85 C the copper steams**, in its sprite and in Hearth Info.

| Copper | From 20 C to 85 C |
|---|---|
| 10 L (full) | about 1.5 hours |
| 5 L | less |
| Banked | holds 50 C, never steams |

> ✏ *Margin note:* a "copper" was the big boiling pot built into a farmhouse's brickwork over its own firebox. Washday, brewing, the pig's mash and the dairy all shared it, which is why so many old kitchens had only the one.

## 6. The Yoghurt Incubator

A lidded clay pot holding 5 L, wrapped in a striped cloth when it's setting. Right-click it:

- **Pour from the Hearth** draws steaming milk from a Hearth within 2 tiles, up to the pot's 5 L.
- **Pour Steamed Milk** takes milk from any container you filled at the copper at 85 C or more. It goes in at the heat it has *now*: a plain container drops to room heat in seconds, a **Wrapped Yoghurt Pot** keeps it hot.
- **Add Starter** takes one vanilla Yoghurt or one Active Yoghurt Starter, before or after the milk. One per batch, whatever the size.
- **Pour Out Milk** empties the pot into a container.

### Cooling, wrapping, setting

1. **Cooling** (lid on): the milk cools towards the temperature of the place, about 85 to 45 C in 2 hours at 20 C. Less than 2.5 L cools as slowly as 2.5 L, so a litre still sets.
2. **The wrap goes on by itself** at 45 C, if a starter is in.
3. **Setting** (cloth on): wrapped, it loses only about 10 C overnight in a 20 C room. It sets in 6 hours at 40 to 45 C, slower when cooler, and stalls below about 18 C. Above 50 C it slows again.

| Milk in the pot | Setting speed |
|---|---|
| below 18 C | stalls |
| 18 to 40 C | slow |
| **40 to 45 C** | **full: 6 hours** |
| 45 to 55 C | slowing, stops at 55 C |

### Too cold

If the milk falls under 40 C with **no starter** in, the pot is **Too cold** and the lid slides half off. Pour it out into a container and back into the copper to reheat, or top the pot up with steaming milk.

Once the starter **is** in, milk that would bring the pot under 40 C is refused, so a cold top-up can never spoil a started batch.

> ✏ *Margin note:* the old test had no thermometer. You cooled the milk until you could hold a finger in it for a count of ten. That's about 45 C, the same point where this pot wraps itself.

## 7. Jars and Cups

When the incubator reads **Ready**:

- **Collect into a Jar**: each empty jar is filled to its size, and the last takes what's left.
- **Fill Empty Yoghurt Containers**: 250 ml each, straight into a vanilla Yoghurt the same age as the batch.

| Container | Holds | Portions of 250 ml | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Glass jar | 1 L | 4 | |
| Clay Jar / Glazed Clay Jar | 2.5 L | 10 | |
| Empty Yoghurt Container | 250 ml | 1 | becomes a vanilla Yoghurt |

A **Jar of Yoghurt** keeps the batch's age, stays fresh 10 days and is rotten at 15. Eat from the jar:

- **Eat a Portion** eats 250 ml, a vanilla Yoghurt's food.
- The game's own **Eat**, **Eat Half** and **Eat Quarter** work too; they eat that share of a full jar.
- **Fill a Yoghurt Cup** / **Fill All Cups** spoons portions into Empty Yoghurt Containers.

The jar gets lighter as you go, its name shows the portions left, and the last spoonful gives the empty jar back.

**The cups go round.** Every vanilla Yoghurt eaten, or stirred into oatmeal, leaves an **Empty Yoghurt Container**.

## 8. The Starter Culture Crock

No yoghurt left to start from? Grow one.

| # | Step | In | Time at its best (25 to 35 C) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Load Wheat and Warm Milk** | 20 wheat grains + 0.5 L milk at 35 to 45 C | |
| 2 | Cultivating | | 24 hours |
| 3 | **Feed Fresh Milk** | 0.5 L of any milk, warm or cold | |
| 4 | Developing | | 12 hours |
| 5 | **Collect 2 Starters** | | 2 Active Yoghurt Starters |

- **The warm milk is the hard part.** It must be 35 to 45 C: from a Hearth within 2 tiles (a copper cooling with the fire out), or carried in a Wrapped Yoghurt Pot. Hotter milk kills the grain's culture, and the menu says why it refuses.
- **An Active Yoghurt Starter** is fresh 5 days and rotten at 10. Each sets one batch.
- **The crock stands on the floor, a kitchen counter or a table**, never on a stove, a fridge or a freezer.

> ✏ *Margin note:* village recipes from the Balkans start yoghurt from a few wheat kernels, a crust of bread, or a twig of cornelian cherry dropped into warm milk. The bacteria that sour milk live on plants and grain too. It's slow and a little uncertain, which is why a household that lost its starter usually just borrowed a spoonful from the neighbours.

## 10. The Dairymaid's Almanac (Seasons & Places)

The milk's temperature follows where the station stands. There are five kinds of place:

| Place | Temperature |
|---|---|
| Outdoors | the day's weather |
| Under a roof | the last 10 days' outdoor average plus 10 C, never above 28 C |
| In a basement | 15 C all year |
| **By a fire** (lit Hearth within 2 tiles) | 28 C |
| By banked embers (banked Hearth within 2 tiles) | 24 C |

Hours for 5 L poured at 85 C at 18:00 with the starter in, **cooling + setting**. "Never" means it hadn't set after 72 hours.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| May | never | 2.0 + 7.7 | 1.8 + 9.2 | 2.5 + 6.2 | 2.2 + 6.3 |

## 11. Secrets & Hidden Features

- ⏱ **It works while you're away**, even in unloaded areas. A pot left for a week reads right when you come back.
- 🔥 **The fire counts only while it would have burned.** Away from a lit Hearth, the pot is "by a fire" until the fuel would have run out, then the room takes over.
- 🥛 **Mixed herds welcome.** Cow's and sheep's milk mix in the copper, a container and the pot, and the blend keeps its shares.
- 🧺 **The Wrapped Yoghurt Pot carries 5 L of hot milk** and loses only about 15 C an hour. Unwrap it when empty for the pot, sheet and twine back.
- 🫙 **Your jar comes back** with the last spoonful.
- 🥄 **Part portions are kept.** Eat Quarter on a jar, and the next cup you fill has exactly what was left.
- 🍶 **It's real vanilla Yoghurt** in the cups, so it works in oatmeal and every recipe that wants yoghurt.

## 11½. The Dairymaid's Notebook

*Kept on a nail by the dairy door, the pages soft with steam.*

### What a pot is worth

| | |
|---|---|
| One pot | 5 L of milk + 1 Yoghurt or starter |
| Makes | 5 L of yoghurt |
| Fills | 5 glass jars, 2 clay jars, or 20 cups |
| Firewood | about 1.5 hours of fire for a full copper, enough for two pots |
| Cooking XP | 10 a pot |

> ✏ *Margin note:* one Yoghurt starts a whole pot here. Real dairies use 3 to 5 spoonfuls in a hundred of milk, about a cup for 5 L. The game is kinder.

### Why it works the way it does

- **Why steam the milk first?** Heating to about 85 C kills whatever else lives in the milk, so the yoghurt bacteria have it to themselves. It also changes the whey proteins, and the yoghurt sets thicker and doesn't weep.
- **Why 45 C?** Yoghurt bacteria like it warmer than most: around 40 to 45 C they work fastest. Much hotter kills them; much cooler and they dawdle, or stop.
- **Why wrap it?** A blanket, a straw box or a place by the embers keeps the pot warm long enough to set overnight. Nobody had to watch it.
- **Why keep one back?** The last batch is the next batch's starter. Each spoonful carries the same culture forward, which is how a family's yoghurt tasted the same for generations.

### Old names, old ways

- **"Yoghurt" is Turkish**, from *yoğurt*, related to a verb for thickening or kneading. English has spelled it yoghurt, yogurt, yoghourt and yaourt.
- **Lactobacillus bulgaricus.** In 1905 a young Bulgarian student, Stamen Grigorov, found the bacterium that sets yoghurt, and it was named after his country.
- **The long-lived peasants.** Ilya Metchnikov, a Nobel prizewinner at the Pasteur Institute, put the long lives of Bulgarian villagers down to their yoghurt, and made it fashionable in Paris and beyond.
- **The copper and the dairy.** In the old farmhouse, the copper boiled the wash on Monday and the milk any day, and the dairy was always the coolest room on the north side.

## 12. Tips

- **Fire up a full copper.** 10 L costs about the same wood as 5 and fills two pots.
- **Set it by the fire in winter.** A lit Hearth within 2 tiles holds a pot at 28 C: about 9 hours from pouring to Ready in any month.
- **Bank the fire overnight.** It holds the copper at 50 C on a third of the wood, and banked embers still keep a pot at 24 C.
- **Keep one yoghurt back. Always.** Otherwise it's a day and a half with the crock.
- **Use the clay jars.** 2.5 L each, 10 portions, and you eat from the jar itself.
- **Save every Empty Yoghurt Container.** They can't be made, only emptied.
- **Grow two starters before you need them.** They're fresh 5 days: start the crock when you're down to your last yoghurt, not after.

## 13. Troubleshooting

| Problem | Why |
|---|---|
| Pour from the Hearth greyed out | The Hearth must stand within 2 tiles of the pot, and its copper must steam (85 C). Keep the fire lit; Hearth Info says when it will be ready |
| Pour Steamed Milk says "Not steamed" | The milk never went through the copper at 85 C or more |
| Pour refused as too cold | The starter is in, and the milk would bring the pot under 40 C. Use hotter milk |
| Pour into the Copper refused, in red | The copper holds one fluid at a time. Only cow's and sheep's milk mix |
| Pot reads **Too cold** | It fell under 40 C before the starter went in. Pour it out, reheat it in the copper, or top it up with steaming milk |
| Setting doesn't move | The pot is below about 18 C. Move it by a fire, or wait for warmer weather |
| Crock won't take the warm milk | It must be 35 to 45 C. The menu says whether it's too hot or too cold |
| Crock won't place | It stands on the floor, a counter or a table, never on a stove, a fridge or a freezer |
| No Empty Yoghurt Containers | They come only from eating Yoghurt. Use jars instead |
| Stations or recipes missing | Check `~/Zomboid/console.txt`. TP Core must be enabled, and the tiledef 5172 must be unique among your mods |

## 15. Appendix: The Tester's Toolkit

*For testers, modders and the curious. These commands exist only when the game is started in debug mode (Steam launch option `-debug`). Type them into the in-game **Command Console** and press Enter. `TPD.help()` lists them in the game; the Hearth's are TP Core's, `TPC.help()`.*

> ⚠ They change your save directly, so try them on a test save, not a playthrough you care about.

Copied from `DEVHELP.md`; change both together.

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `TPD.weather()` | Temperature by place, and how fast milk sets at each. |
| `TPD.here()` | Which kind of place you're standing in. |
| `TPD.pots()` | Every loaded incubator: stage, litres, milk temperature, what's next. |
| `TPD.skip(hours)` | Moves every loaded incubator on by this many hours. |
| `TPD.fast(hours)` | Test timer for setting. `TPD.fast()` goes back to the sandbox time. |
| `TPD.milk(litres, c)` | A cooking pot of cow's milk (default 5 L), at c degrees if given. |
| `TPD.kit(kind)` | What a test needs: yoghurt (default), `"crock"` or `"wrap"`; `"?"` lists them. |
| `TPD.crocks()` | Every loaded crock: stage, progress, place. |
| `TPD.culture(hours)` | Moves every loaded crock's current stage on by this many hours. |
| `TPD.tiles()` | Every station within 3 tiles: object class, sprite, solid flags. |

**Typical check:** within 2 tiles of a Hearth, `TPC.copper(5)` and `TPC.hot()`, then `TPD.kit()`, Pour from the Hearth and Add Starter. `TPD.skip(3)` should show it wrapped and setting, `TPD.skip(7)` Ready.
