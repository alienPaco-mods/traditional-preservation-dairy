## 1. What This Mod Does

In vanilla, yogurt comes only from fridges, and once the town is picked clean it's gone. Your cow or sheep keeps giving milk, but there's nothing to do with it except drink it or churn it.

This mod lets you **set your own yogurt the old way**: milk brought to the steam in a Stone Hearth's cauldron, cooled in a lidded clay pot, then wrapped in cloth to set overnight. The result is ordinary vanilla **Yogurt**, so it works in every recipe that uses it. Every Yogurt eaten leaves its cup behind to fill again, and when the last spoonful of starter is gone, a crock of wheat and warm milk grows a new one.

The same Culturing Pot makes **kefir**: grains found on farm shelves, put in milk and left a day, no fire needed. Kefir is a drink that eases food sickness, and the grains grow with every batch (chapter 10).

## 4. Walkthrough

1. **Keep a cow or sheep in milk.** Cow's and sheep's milk both work, and mix.
2. **Build a Stone Hearth** from the Build menu, forge a **Forged Cauldron** and **Fit Cauldron** on it (TP Core's guide, chapter 4), and stack firewood by it.
3. **Make the Lidded Clay Pot** on the pottery wheel and fire it in a kiln.
4. **Build the Culturing Pot** from the Build menu, within 2 tiles of the Stone Hearth.
5. **Pour into the Cauldron** (right-click the Stone Hearth) from your milk buckets, then **Light** the fire.
6. Check the **Stone Hearth Info** window. When the cauldron reads "steaming", the milk is at 85 C. **Keep it steaming for 20 minutes**: then the cauldron's milk reads *Scalded* and will pour.
7. Right-click the Culturing Pot: **Pour from the Cauldron**, then **Add Starter** with a Yogurt or a Jar of Yogurt you kept back.
8. Go to bed. The lid stays on while it cools, and at 45 C the cloth wraps itself on.
9. In the morning the status reads **Ready**. **Collect into a Jar** or **Fill Empty Yogurt Containers**.
10. Keep one Yogurt back for the next batch.

That's it. A full cauldron is twelve pots, 60 L of yogurt from one firing.

**No cauldron yet?** Any pot of milk on a lit stove, fireplace, barbecue or campfire scalds once it has had 20 minutes at 85 C, as vanilla boils tainted water clean. Take it off and pour it straight into the Culturing Pot (**Pour Scalded Milk**) while it's hot: an open pot loses its heat in minutes. Pour it into a **Wrapped Clay Pot**, or any pot, jar or bucket you've wrapped in a sheet, first if the walk is long. The cauldron's gifts are its size, pouring straight into a pot or crock within 2 tiles, and a banked fire's steady low heat.

## 5. The Forged Cauldron

The Stone Hearth is TP Core's station: a vanilla fireplace (Add Fuel, Light, cooking, room heat). With TP Core's **Forged Cauldron** fitted, a 60 L iron kettle hangs over its hole on a chain. Right-click it for **Pour into the Cauldron**, **Fill from the Cauldron** (into your own containers), **Empty the Cauldron**, **Bank the Fire** / **Stoke the Fire** and **Stone Hearth Info**.

- **One fluid at a time.** Cow's and sheep's milk count as one: they mix freely, and a blend keeps its shares in the cauldron, in a container and in the pot. Any other mix is refused, shown in red with the reason.
- **The fire heats the cauldron towards 95 C**, and milk is kept off the boil. Cold milk poured onto hot cools the lot.
- **At 85 C the cauldron steams**, in its look and in the Stone Hearth window, and you hear it simmer. After **20 minutes at the steam** (in all) its milk reads **Scalded**. Pour more milk in and the 20 minutes start over.

> *Margin note:* the old dairywoman's rule was to let the pot "stand at the steam" while she said her prayers, or about as long as it takes to scrub the churn. That quarter of an hour or more is what turns the whey proteins so the yogurt sets firm instead of runny. A thermometer only tells you when to start counting.

| Cauldron | From 20 C to 85 C |
|---|---|
| 60 L (full) | about 2¾ hours |
| 20 L | about 2 hours |
| 10 L | about 1.5 hours |
| 5 L | about 1¼ hours |
| Banked | holds 50 C, never steams |

The Stone Hearth and its cauldron have their own guide, **Traditional Preservation: Core** (number 6), with forging and fitting the cauldron, banking, the places by a fire and the full tables.

## 6. The Culturing Pot

A lidded clay pot holding 5 L, wrapped in a striped cloth when it's setting. Right-click it:

- **Pour from the Cauldron** draws steaming, scalded milk from a Stone Hearth within 2 tiles, up to the pot's 5 L. Before its 20 minutes are up the option is greyed and says how many minutes are left.
- **Pour Scalded Milk** takes **scalded** milk from any container: milk that has been held at 85 C or more for 20 minutes, in the cauldron or in a pot on a stove or a fire, or store milk poured out of its carton (it was pasteurised). It goes in at the heat it has *now*: off the heat, an open pot or bucket cools in minutes (85 to 45 C in about a quarter hour), a **Wrapped Clay Pot** in hours. Pouring between containers carries the heat over, so a pot just off the stove poured into a Wrapped Pot stays hot.
- **Add Starter** takes one vanilla Yogurt, one portion from a Jar of Yogurt (250 ml, a cup's worth: the rest stays in the jar, and the empty jar comes back with the last), or one Active Yogurt Starter, before or after the milk. None of them gone off. One per batch, whatever the size.
- **Pour Out Milk** empties the pot into a container.

### Cooling, wrapping, setting

1. **Cooling** (lid on): the milk cools towards the temperature of the place, about 85 to 45 C in 2 hours at 20 C. Less than 2.5 L cools as slowly as 2.5 L, so a litre still sets.
2. **The wrap goes on by itself** at 45 C, if a starter is in.
3. **Setting** (cloth on): wrapped, it loses only about 10 C overnight in a 20 C room. It sets in 6 hours at 40 to 45 C, slower when cooler, and stalls below about 18 C. Above 45 C it slows again, and stops at 55 C.

| Milk in the pot | Setting speed |
|---|---|
| below 18 C | stalls |
| 18 to 40 C | slow |
| **40 to 45 C** | **full: 6 hours** |
| 45 to 55 C | slowing, stops at 55 C |

### Too cold

If the milk falls under 40 C with **no starter** in, the pot is **Too cold** and the lid slides half off. Pour it out into a container and reheat it in the cauldron or on a stove, or top the pot up with steaming milk.

Once the starter **is** in, milk that would bring the pot under 40 C is refused, so a cold top-up can never spoil a started batch.

> *Margin note:* the old test had no thermometer. You cooled the milk until you could hold a finger in it for a count of ten. That's about 45 C, the same point where this pot wraps itself.

## 7. Jars and Cups

When the Culturing Pot reads **Ready**:

- **Collect into a Jar**: each empty jar is filled to its size, and the last takes what's left.
- **Fill Empty Yogurt Containers**: 250 ml each, straight into a vanilla Yogurt the same age as the batch.

| Container | Holds | Portions of 250 ml | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Glass jar | 1 L | 4 | |
| Ceramic Jar / Glazed Ceramic Jar | 2.5 L | 10 | |
| Empty Yogurt Container | 250 ml | 1 | becomes a vanilla Yogurt |

A **Jar of Yogurt** keeps the batch's age, stays fresh 10 days and is rotten at 15. Eat from the jar:

- **Eat a Portion** eats 250 ml, with the food of the 250 ml of milk it was made from.
- The game's own **Eat**, **Eat Half** and **Eat Quarter** work too; they eat that share of a full jar.
- **Fill a Yogurt Cup** / **Fill All Cups** spoons portions into Empty Yogurt Containers.

The jar gets lighter as you go, its name shows the portions left, and the last spoonful gives the empty jar back.

**Yogurt is the milk, set.** Nothing is lost in the pot, so a portion has a quarter of a litre of its milk's food,
and a cup filled from the pot or the jar has the same. A mixed pot goes by its shares.

| 250 ml portion of | Hunger | Calories | Carbs | Fat | Protein |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cow's milk yogurt | -12.5 | 154 | 11.5 g | 5.5 g | 8.25 g |
| Sheep's milk yogurt | -12.5 | 206 | 5.75 g | 4.25 g | 5.75 g |

**Shop yogurt is cow's milk yogurt too**, so a vanilla Yogurt found from now on has the cow's figures (vanilla's
are 30 calories and -10 hunger). Yogurt already in your world when you add the mod keeps vanilla's figures until
eaten. A Yogurt stirred into oatmeal now brings its fuller food with it.

**The cups go round.** Every vanilla Yogurt eaten, or stirred into oatmeal, leaves an **Empty Yogurt Container**.

## 8. When Milk and Butter Go Off

Vanilla milk keeps for ever. With this mod it doesn't, and what you do with a bucket of milk in its first day decides what it's good for.

| The milk reads | When | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| **Cow's Milk (Fresh)** | Raw and young | Everything |
| **Cow's Milk (Scalded, Fresh)** | It has been held at 85 C or more for 20 minutes (the cauldron, or a pot on a lit stove or fire), or it came out of the Culturing Pot or a store carton | Everything. It never sours |
| **Cow's Milk (Soured)** | Raw milk, a day and a half old in a warm room | Drinking (the same food, a little unhappiness), baking (cake batter, muffins, cereal) and starting the crock without wheat (chapter 9). Not yogurt, butter or mildew cure |
| **Cow's Milk (Rotten)** | Any milk, twice that old | Nothing. It poisons you: pour it away |

Sheep's milk and the game's plain "animal's milk" go the same way under their own names.

| Kept at | Raw milk sours in | Any milk is rotten in |
|---|---|---|
| 30 C | 1 day | 2 days |
| 20 C, a room in July | 1½ to 2 days | 3 days |
| A basement in summer, about 13 C | 3½ days | 7 days |
| A basement in winter, about 5 C | 6 to 7 days | about 2 weeks |
| A powered fridge | about 8 days | about 16 days |
| A powered freezer | never | never |

- **Scalding buys you the first half.** Scalded milk skips souring and stays good until the day raw milk would be rotten.
- **Pouring never makes milk young.** Mix two lots and the older age wins. Raw milk poured into scalded makes the whole lot raw again.
- **A fresh milking into an emptied bucket starts afresh.**
- **Sealed store milk doesn't age** until you pour it out. Then it counts as scalded: it was pasteurised.
- **Milk in the cauldron ages too**, and 20 minutes of steaming scald whatever is in it.
- **Any milk bakes.** Cake batter, muffins and a bowl of cereal take cow's, sheep's or animal's milk, fresh, scalded or soured. Butter and mildew cure take their milk fresh or scalded.
- It keeps counting while you're away, by the place the milk was left in.

> *Margin note:* raw milk carries its own lactic bacteria, which is why it sours on the shelf: that's clabber, the old baker's buttermilk. Scalding kills them, so scalded milk has nothing to sour it and simply goes bad in the end.

Both can be changed in the sandbox: **Milk goes off** and **Milk spoiling speed**. Milk also follows the game's own **Food Spoilage** setting, as every food does: on Very Slow it keeps 2.5 times as long, and Milk spoiling speed multiplies on top.

### Clabber or yogurt?

Soured milk and yogurt are both milk turned by lactic bacteria. They're not the same bacteria, and they don't like the same heat.

| | Clabber (soured milk) | Yogurt |
|---|---|---|
| Who does the work | Mesophilic bacteria: *Lactococcus*, carried in raw milk | Thermophilic bacteria: *S. thermophilus* and *L. bulgaricus*, from a starter |
| Happiest at | 20 to 30 C, a warm kitchen | 40 to 45 C, a wrapped pot |
| Started by | Nothing: raw milk left alone | A spoonful of the last batch |
| Scalded milk | Never clabbers: the heat killed them | Wants it: the heat cleared the way |
| Here it's good for | Drinking, baking, a quick start for the crock | Eating, cooking, the next batch |

- **Load Soured Milk** puts half a litre of clabber into an empty Starter Culture Crock, warm or cold, with no wheat. It skips the grain's day of cultivating and waits for fresh milk at once: **starters in about 12 hours instead of 36.**
- **Rotten milk won't do.** By then other things are living in it.
- **Backslopping** is the old word for starting a batch with a little of the last one. Every yogurt you set from a cup of the last pot is backslopped, and so is every crock you start from soured milk.

### Butter

Vanilla butter keeps for ever. Here it goes **rancid**, as real butter does: fresh for about a month in a cool cellar, much less in a warm kitchen. Store butter and churned butter alike.

| Kept at | Butter fresh / rotten | Salted Butter fresh / rotten |
|---|---|---|
| A cellar or basement, about 15 C | 30 / 45 days | 90 / 120 days |
| A powered fridge | about 75 / 110 days | about 7 / 10 months |
| A room at 20 C | about 20 / 30 days | about 60 / 80 days |
| 25 C and over | about 15 / 22 days | about 45 / 60 days |
| Frozen, or a powered freezer | never | never |

Two old ways to make it last. Both need **Cooking 2**.

- **Salt Butter** (right-click the butter): a **Butter** and **1 Salt** (one use of anything salt: a box of Salt does ten butters). You get a **Salted Butter**: three times the keeping, the same food. A half-stale butter makes a half-stale salted one, so salt it fresh.
- **Clarify it.** Right-click a butter, **Put Butter in a Pot**, pick the pot, then **One** (the oldest butter) or **All**: each butter melts to a quarter litre of **Butter (Melting)**, as many as there is room for (a Pot or Forged Pot takes 6, a Saucepan or Copper Saucepan 2; more can go in until it is clarified, and that starts the hour again). Keep it steaming an hour on any stove or fire and the whole pot is **Clarified Butter (Melted)**. **Strain Clarified Butter** through a cloth (Cheese Cloth, Dish Towel, Ripped Sheets, a Sheet or a Strainer, kept): a quarter litre to a **Clarified Butter**. **It never goes off**, like Lard. Salted butter clarifies too.
- **Mind the pot.** Another hour on the heat and the butter burns: you are left with a burnt pot, and **Scrape Out Burnt Butter** gives the pot back. Raw melting butter can't be poured into another container or topped up with anything else (Fluid, **Empty** throws it away if you change your mind), and left off the heat it goes rancid when its oldest butter would have.
- **A whole summer's butter at once** goes in the Stone Hearth's cauldron, the same way at a bigger size: **Put Butter in the Cauldron** takes one or all of the Butter and Salted Butter you carry. Keep it steaming an hour over a lit fire, fill a pot or bucket from the cauldron and strain it. It can't burn in the cauldron, but left there raw it goes rancid just the same.

| | Butter | Salted Butter | Clarified Butter |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weight | 0.3 | 0.3 | 0.25 |
| Calories | 3,200 | 3,200 | 3,200 |
| Keeps in a cellar | a month | three months | for ever |
| Cooks like | Butter | Butter | Butter: stews, soups, pasta, toast, baking |

With **Fish & Meat** on, Potted Meat seals under Clarified Butter as it does under Lard.

> *Margin note:* fresh butter is still about a sixth water, with a little milk in it, and that is where it spoils first. Salt in the water holds the bacteria back, which is why Irish firkin butter, packed heavy with salt, crossed the Atlantic in casks. Boil the water off, pour the fat from the milk solids, and little is left to spoil: Indian *ghee* and Egyptian *samna* kept through a hot summer in a ceramic jar.

> *Margin note:* in Russia soured raw milk is *prostokvasha*, "simply soured", and the Irish called it *bainne clábair*, "thick milk", which English made into bonny clabber. Every farm kitchen had a jug of it going, for pancakes and soda bread. The crock here takes a little game licence: a real clabber won't turn into yogurt by itself. Fed warm fresh milk, though, its acid and bacteria give the crock the head start the wheat would take a day to grow.

### Frost

Everything in this mod freezes at **0 C**. A short frost does nothing; **six hours frozen in all** freeze a thing through, and it changes once:

| Left out in the frost | After 6 hours frozen in all |
|---|---|
| **Kefir Grains**, loose, wet on a Drying Cloth, or in a kefir pot | Live ones go **Kefir Grains (Hungry)**. One milk change brings them back |
| **Kefir**, in a jug or ready in the pot | **Kefir (Separated)**, as if three days old |
| **Yogurt**, **Jar of Yogurt** | **Yogurt (Thawed)**: +10 unhappiness, once. The food is all still there |
| **Active Yogurt Starter** | Each frozen hour wears it down: two days of frost in all and it is spent |

- **Safe:** Dried Kefir Grains, milk of every kind, Butter, Salted Butter and Clarified Butter. Milk setting in the Culturing Pot and the crock's culture only wait for the thaw.
- **Never freezes:** a basement, a place by a lit fire or banked embers, a powered fridge. A powered freezer is the game's own business. Cooked food never freezes.
- Sandbox **Frost harms** turns it off.

> *Margin note:* ice crystals tear the curd, so thawed yogurt weeps whey and turns grainy, though it is as nourishing as before. Kefir grains and yogurt bacteria mostly live through a freezing, only slowed; dried, the grains don't mind it at all.

## 9. The Starter Culture Crock

No yogurt left to start from? Grow one.

| # | Step | In | Time at its best (25 to 35 C) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Load Wheat and Warm Milk** | 20 wheat grains + 0.5 L milk at 35 to 45 C | |
| 2 | Cultivating | | 24 hours |
| 1-2 | *or* **Load Soured Milk** | 0.5 L soured milk, any warmth, no wheat | none: on to step 3 |
| 3 | **Feed Fresh Milk** | 0.5 L of any milk, warm or cold | |
| 4 | Developing | | 12 hours |
| 5 | **Collect 2 Starters** | | 2 Active Yogurt Starters |

- **The warm milk is the hard part.** It must be 35 to 45 C: from a Stone Hearth within 2 tiles (a cauldron cooling with the fire out), or carried in any container at that heat (a Wrapped Clay Pot holds it there longest). Hotter milk kills the grain's culture, and the menu says why it refuses.
- **Soured milk is the short way.** Half a litre of it, warm or cold, starts the crock with no wheat and goes straight to Feed Fresh Milk. Rotten milk is refused. See *Clabber or yogurt?* in chapter 8.
- **An Active Yogurt Starter** is fresh 5 days and rotten at 10. Each sets one batch.
- **The crock stands on the floor, a kitchen counter or a table**, never on a stove, a fridge or a freezer.

> *Margin note:* village recipes from the Balkans start yogurt from a few wheat kernels, a crust of bread, or a twig of cornelian cherry dropped into warm milk. The bacteria that sour milk live on plants and grain too. It's slow and a little uncertain, which is why a household that lost its starter usually just borrowed a spoonful from the neighbours.

## 10. Kefir

Kefir is milk turned by **grains**: little white lumps, like boiled cauliflower, of bacteria and yeasts living together. Put them in milk, leave it a day in a room, strain them out and put them in the next milk. No fire, no wrap, no thermometer. It is made in the same **Culturing Pot**, one culture at a time: a pot holds yogurt or kefir, never both. The starter decides: milk in the pot with no yogurt starter yet takes grains instead once it is 45 C or cooler (too cold for a yogurt starter, below 40 C, it can only go to kefir).

The grains are what you keep. They grow with every good batch, starve out of milk, and can't be made by any recipe: lose them all and you are back to searching the shelves.

### Finding grains

A **Packet of Kefir Grains** turns up on a farm's storage shelves and a Gigamart's farming shelf, very rarely in a home fridge or a kitchen cupboard. **Open Packet of Kefir Grains** (crafting menu, as a seed packet) for **10 Dried Kefir Grains**.

| Item | Weight | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Packet of Kefir Grains | 0.02 | A sachet, torn open for 10 Dried Kefir Grains |
| Dried Kefir Grains | 0.001 | Asleep. They keep for ever, and 2 batches in milk bring them back |
| Kefir Grains | 0.001 | One grain, a gram. Its state is in its name. Not food |
| Kefir Grains on a Cloth | 0.1 and up | An older save's grains spread to dry; today's dry on the Drying Cloth |

### A batch

| # | Step | Where | In | Out | Time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Add Kefir Grains** | An empty Culturing Pot, or its milk with no yogurt starter at 45 C or cooler | Grains of one state, **One** or **All** | A kefir pot | a moment |
| 2 | **Pour Milk** | Kefir pot | Any milk, raw or scalded, cow's or sheep's, from a container or a Stone Hearth's cauldron within 2 tiles, up to 5 L, under 45 C | Fermenting, under a linen cap | a moment |
| 3 | Ferment | Culturing Pot | | 5 L of Kefir (Fresh) | **24 hours** at 18 to 25 C with a live grain to every 40 ml* |
| 4 | **Strain Kefir** | Culturing Pot, ready | Empty containers; a Strainer, or a cloth (Cheesecloth, Dish Cloth, Ripped Sheet, Sheet) at twice the time, kept | Kefir, and all the grains on the first strain | as pouring |

\* 125 live grains for a full 5 L pot. A packet's 10 dried grains count half while they come back: 200 ml in a day, then 400 ml once live.

- **Grains go in first.** Grains into an empty pot make it a kefir pot; a pot with yogurt milk or a starter in refuses them. More grains can go in while it ferments.
- **The milk can be cold.** Straight from the cow, the fridge or the cauldron, as it is. Only heat hurts: milk over **45 C** is refused, and at **35 to 45 C** the live grains come out Hungry (the menu warns you).
- **Fewer grains, slower kefir.** Speed is the grains' share of the full ratio: 25 live grains in 5 L work at a fifth of the speed, 5 days at 20 C. More than the full ratio go no faster. Pouring more milk in mid-batch dilutes what is done.
- **Take Out Kefir Grains** gives them back before the milk goes in.
- **Mixed milks** ferment by their shares. Animal's milk (from Milk Powder) makes cow's kefir.

> *Margin note:* kefir grains are not grain at all. They are a rubbery web of milk protein and a sugar called kefiran, spun by thirty-odd kinds of bacteria and yeasts that live in it together. The yeasts are why kefir fizzes a little and carries a trace of alcohol, usually under 1 per cent.

### How fast

| Place temperature | Speed | 5 L at the full ratio |
|---|---|---|
| frozen | paused | |
| 5 C (a basement in winter) | 0.18 | about 5½ days |
| 10 C | 0.35 | about 3 days |
| 15 C | 0.6 | about 40 hours |
| **18 to 25 C** | **1** | **24 hours** |
| 28 C (by a fire indoors) | 1.3 | about 18.5 hours |
| 30 to 35 C | 1.5 | 16 hours |

The pot has no wrap: it takes the temperature of where it stands (the Almanac, chapter 12). Kefir is the easy one in summer and the hard one in winter, when a room drops under 10 C.

### The grains, batch to batch

Each grain counts by its state, and its name says which:

| Grains | Count for speed | Grow | After the batch |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Kefir Grains (Live)** | 1 | a tenth more a batch, for the share fermented at 15 to 28 C | Live |
| **Kefir Grains (Hungry)** | half | no | Live |
| **Dried Kefir Grains**, then **Kefir Grains (Reviving)** | half | no | a step nearer Live: 2 batches from dried |
| **Kefir Grains (Dead)** | refused | | |

A part-grain of growth stays with the pot for the next batch, so 25 grains don't need ten batches to make one.

### Grains out of milk

Grains starve without milk: carried, in a crate, in a pot with no milk, or left in a finished batch from the moment it is ready. Each grain keeps its own clock, so grains from one strain can be split any way: some back in the pot, some to the fridge, some to dry.

| Kept at | Hungry in | Dead in |
|---|---|---|
| 30 C | about 1⅓ days | about 5 days |
| **20 C, a room** | **2 days** | **7 days** |
| A basement in summer, about 13 C | about 4½ days | about 16 days |
| A powered fridge | about 10 days | about 5 weeks |
| Frozen | they don't starve, but 6 hours frozen turn live ones Hungry | they wait |

A place over **35 C** turns live grains Hungry, over **45 C** kills them. Grains coming back from dried don't go Hungry, but die at 7 days just the same.

### Drying grains

For keeping, dry them on a **Drying Cloth** (TP Core: with a Sheet and 2 Stones, right-click the floor, **Spread Drying Cloth**). Right-click it: **Lay Kefir Grains on the Cloth**: a cloth takes fifty grains, or fewer beside yeast cakes. Lay it where the air moves: in a shut room they wait for a window.

| Place | Dry in |
|---|---|
| 25 C and over | 2 days |
| 20 C | 2½ days |
| 10 C | 5 days |
| 5 C | 10 days |
| frozen | paused |

When the **Drying Cloth Info** window says Dry, **Take Off the Finished Lots**: Dried Kefir Grains. Taken off early, they come back live. Dead grains aren't laid.

### Kefir

Kefir is a fluid, poured, drunk and carried as milk is. It is its milk less the sugar the grains ate:

| A litre of | Hunger | Thirst | Unhappiness | Calories | Carbs | Fat | Protein |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cow's milk | -50 | -50 | | 615 | 46 g | 22 g | 33 g |
| **Kefir (Fresh)**, cow's | -50 | -50 | | 590 | 40 g | 22 g | 33 g |
| **Kefir (Fresh)**, sheep's | -50 | -50 | | 798 | 17 g | 17 g | 23 g |
| **Kefir (Separated)** | -50 | -40 | +10 | as fresh | | | |
| **Kefir (Rotten)** | | -20 | +30 | none: a mild poison | | | |

- **Fresh kefir settles the stomach.** Each litre drunk while you have food sickness takes **10** off it. Then not again for **3 game hours**. Drunk healthy, it does nothing and starts no wait.
- **It ages from the moment it is ready**, in the pot or out, on milk's curve:

| Kept at | Separates in | Rotten in |
|---|---|---|
| 30 C | 2 days | about 7 days |
| **20 C** | **3 days** | **10 days** |
| A basement in summer, about 13 C | about 7 days | about 3 weeks |
| A powered fridge | about 16 days | about 7 weeks |
| A powered freezer | never | never |

- **Separated kefir bakes** as soured milk does: cake batter, muffins and cereal take it in place of milk.
- Cow's and sheep's kefir mix, and the jug reads plain Kefir.

> *Margin note:* the story goes that the Caucasus mountaineers called the grains the Grains of the Prophet and would not sell them. In 1908 a Moscow dairy sent a young woman, Irina Sakharova, to charm a few from a Caucasian prince; he refused, his men carried her off to marry him, and a Tsar's court settled the matter by awarding her ten pounds of grains. Moscow had its kefir soon after. True or not, it tells you what the grains were worth.

## 13. Secrets & Hidden Features

- **It works while you're away**, even in unloaded areas. A pot left for a week reads right when you come back.
- **The Wrapped Clay Pot carries 5 L of hot milk** and loses only about 15 C an hour. Unwrap it when empty for the pot, sheet and twine back.
- **Any pot, jar or bucket can be wrapped** the same way and keeps its own size: a wrapped bucket carries 10 L and holds its heat longest, a wrapped jar only a litre and cools sooner.
- **Never put a wrapped pot on the heat.** The sheet and twine catch within a minute. On a stove, in an oven or on a barbecue the kitchen goes up with them; in a fireplace, a campfire or the Stone Hearth they just burn away.
- **Part portions are kept.** Eat Quarter on a jar, and the next cup you fill has exactly what was left.

## 14. The Dairymaid's Notebook

*Kept on a nail by the dairy door, the pages soft with steam.*

### What a pot is worth

| | |
|---|---|
| One pot | 5 L of milk + 1 Yogurt or starter |
| Makes | 5 L of yogurt |
| Fills | 5 glass jars, 2 ceramic jars, or 20 cups |
| Cooking XP | 10 a pot |

> *Margin note:* one Yogurt starts a whole pot here. Real dairies use 3 to 5 spoonfuls in a hundred of milk, about a cup for 5 L. The game is kinder.

### Why it works the way it does

- **Why steam the milk first?** Heating to about 85 C kills whatever else lives in the milk, so the yogurt bacteria have it to themselves. It also changes the whey proteins, and the yogurt sets thicker and doesn't weep.
- **Why 45 C?** Yogurt bacteria like it warmer than most: around 40 to 45 C they work fastest. Much hotter kills them; much cooler and they dawdle, or stop.
- **Why wrap it?** A blanket, a straw box or a place by the embers keeps the pot warm long enough to set overnight. Nobody had to watch it.
- **Why keep one back?** The last batch is the next batch's starter. Each spoonful carries the same culture forward, which is how a family's yogurt tasted the same for generations.

### Old names, old ways

- **"Yogurt" is Turkish**, from *yoğurt*, related to a verb for thickening or kneading. English has spelled it yogurt, yoghurt, yoghourt and yaourt.
- **Lactobacillus bulgaricus.** In 1905 a young Bulgarian student, Stamen Grigorov, found the bacterium that sets yogurt, and it was named after his country.
- **The long-lived peasants.** Ilya Metchnikov, a Nobel prizewinner at the Pasteur Institute, put the long lives of Bulgarian villagers down to their yogurt, and made it fashionable in Paris and beyond.
- **The dairy** was always the coolest room on the north side of the old farmhouse; the milk was boiled in the copper (TP Core's guide has its story).

## 15. Tips

- **Set it by the fire in winter.** A lit Stone Hearth, fireplace or antique stove within 2 tiles indoors holds a pot at 28 C: about 8 and a half hours from pouring to Ready in any month. A porch fire still sets a January pot in about 10 hours; a campfire in the open doesn't.
- **Keep one yogurt back. Always.** A portion of a jar will do. Otherwise it's a day and a half with the crock, or half a day if you have soured milk.
- **Soured milk isn't waste.** Bake with it, or start the crock with it.
- **Use the ceramic jars.** 2.5 L each, 10 portions, and you eat from the jar itself.
- **Save every Empty Yogurt Container.** They can't be made, only emptied.
- **Clarify the summer's butter.** A cow gives most in summer, when butter keeps worst. Clarified, it waits for winter in any cupboard.
- **Grow two starters before you need them.** They're fresh 5 days: start the crock when you're down to your last yogurt, not after.
- **Never keep all your kefir grains in one pot.** Dry a handful on a cloth as soon as you have spare: dried, they keep for ever.
- **Grow the grains before you drink the kefir.** A packet's 10 grains take about four weeks of daily batches (two to wake, then a tenth more each) to reach the 125 a full pot wants. Keep each batch small, at the full ratio, so it is done in a day.
- **Keep a jug of kefir for the bad days.** A litre takes 10 off food sickness, once every 3 hours.
